SEAMEC in One Page

A Mumbai-headquartered offshore oilfield services company that owns and operates a fleet of specialised Diving Support Vessels (DSVs), an Offshore Support Vessel (OSV), and an Accommodation Barge.

46.1%

The Business in One Sentence

SEAMEC charters specialised offshore vessels — primarily DSVs — on day-rate contracts to oil & gas operators and their contractors, earning revenue as a function of vessels × utilisation × day rate × operating days, with a largely fixed cost base that creates significant operating leverage.

FY2025-26 in One Sentence

Revenue rose 46.1% to ₹901.94 Cr and PAT rose 109.7% to ₹242.35 Cr, driven by higher deployment days (1,859 vs. 1,454), new contracts at premium day rates, and the acquisition of SEAMEC AGASTYA — but free cash flow turned negative due to heavy capex.

⚓ The Central Investment Question

Are the current high day rates and near-full utilisation structural (driven by global DSV scarcity — only ~58 high-spec vessels worldwide, with only 2 new builds vs. mid-single-digit retirements over 3 years) or cyclical (driven by a temporary spike in offshore activity)? Management's actions — fleet rejuvenation, long-term ONGC contracts via HAL, and the USD 70 million (₹674.57 Cr) ANANT acquisition — suggest they believe the scarcity thesis is structural.

01
Business Fundamentals
Part 01

What Does SEAMEC Actually Do?

Explained from first principles — starting from the customer and working backwards to the vessel.

TL;DR SEAMEC owns specialised DSVs that oil & gas operators hire on day-rate contracts to inspect, maintain, and repair subsea infrastructure. The customer is ONGC (directly or via HAL), and the work is essential IMR that cannot be done by ordinary vessels.

Who Are SEAMEC's Customers?

  • 01 Oil & gas E&P companies — primarily ONGC, India's largest upstream company.
  • 02 Large EPC and marine contractors — L&T, Safeen Al Behar (UAE), Mubarak Marine (UAE), A D Engineers.
  • 03 International oil companies — Saudi Aramco, UAE operators.

What Problem Are They Solving?

Underwater inspection, maintenance, and repair (IMR) of offshore oil & gas infrastructure — subsea pipelines, wellheads, platforms, and mooring systems. Without it, corrosion and structural degradation can lead to catastrophic failures and production loss.

What Exactly Does a Diving Support Vessel (DSV) Do?

A DSV is a floating base for professional divers and ROVs. It has a moonpool — a hole in the middle of the vessel open to the sea — through which divers, ROVs, and equipment pass to and from the worksite.

It uses Dynamic Positioning (DP) — computer-controlled thrusters with DGPS and transponder inputs — to maintain precise station over a dive site, compensating for swell and currents.

DSVs are equipped for saturation diving — divers live in a pressurised habitat on the vessel and are transferred to the worksite in a diving bell, allowing extended work at depth without repeated decompression.

They carry ROVs for unmanned subsea operations, and heavy cranes and winches for subsea construction.

Typical Offshore Project Flow

  1. Survey & prep — Operator identifies subsea infrastructure needing work.
  2. Vessel mobilisation — DSV moves to field, often after drydock.
  3. Offshore ops — DSV maintains station; divers/ROVs work. Days to months.
  4. Demobilisation — Work completed; vessel moves on.

Why Not Use an Ordinary Vessel?

  • ✕ Cannot maintain precise station over subsea worksite.
  • ✕ Cannot support saturation diving.
  • ✕ Cannot launch/recover ROVs through moonpool.
  • ✕ Cannot handle heavy subsea construction equipment.

Ownership & Contract Flow

The offshore asset is owned by the operator (ONGC). The contract is awarded by the operator or a main EPC contractor.

In SEAMEC's case, significant domestic work flows through HAL Offshore (promoter), which holds the prime ONGC contract and subcontracts vessels from SEAMEC.

💰 Implied Economics

At USD 45,000/day and, say, 300 operating days/year, annual gross charter revenue would be ~USD 13.5 million (~₹113 Cr). Against an acquisition cost of USD 70 million (~₹674.57 Cr), that implies a gross yield of ~16.8% on acquisition cost. Even after operating costs (crew, fuel, insurance, maintenance), the vessel should generate strong cash flows. The key risk is utilisation — if the ONGC contract runs for only part of the year, returns are lower.

Is This Transformational?

It is material but not transformational — it adds one high-spec DSV to a fleet of 8, increasing capacity by ~12%. However, at USD 45,000/day (nearly double the AGASTYA rate of USD 25,000/day), it signals that SEAMEC is moving up the value chain toward higher-spec, higher-rate assets.

Why Did Margins Expand?

01 · OPERATING LEVERAGE

Revenue grew 46.1% while total expenses grew only 36.6%, because vessel costs (crew, insurance, depreciation) are largely fixed.

Industry Introduction

The offshore oilfield services industry — in plain English, before the jargon.

What Industry Does SEAMEC Operate In?

SEAMEC operates in the offshore oilfield services industry, specifically the niche segment of Diving Support Vessels (DSVs) and Multi Support Vessels (MSVs).

Position in the Value Chain

SEAMEC sits downstream of oil & gas E&P companies. When ONGC or an international oil company develops or maintains an offshore field, they hire vessels for subsea construction, inspection, and repair. SEAMEC provides the vessels and marine crew.

Demand Drivers

  • ▸ Oil prices — Higher prices encourage E&P spending.
  • ▸ Offshore development — New fields require DSV support.
  • ▸ Ageing infrastructure — Existing pipelines need inspection.
  • ▸ Offshore wind — Emerging demand for DSVs.

Supply Constraints

The global high-spec DSV fleet is structurally supply-constrained — only ~58 vessels worldwide, with only 2 new builds vs. mid-single-digit retirements expected over 3 years. Some NOCs restrict vessels older than 20 years from IMR work, further shrinking the addressable fleet.

Cyclicality & Contracts

The industry is highly cyclical, tied to oil & gas capex cycles. Contracts are typically time-charter contracts (day-rate based) ranging from a few months to 5+ years, with options for extension.

What I Should Understand Before I Even Think About Valuation

15 concepts that must be understood before looking at the stock price.

  1. This is a charter-hire business — Revenue = vessels × utilisation × day rate × operating days. Small changes in any variable have outsized impact.
  2. DSVs are specialised, scarce assets — Only ~58 high-spec vessels globally. New supply is constrained. This scarcity is the core of the bull case.
  3. The business has high operating leverage — Costs are largely fixed. When utilisation and day rates rise, margins expand rapidly. When they fall, margins collapse.
  4. Related-party transactions are pervasive — SEAMEC buys vessels from HAL, charters vessels to HAL, pays rent to a director's spouse, and pays management fees to MMG Advisors.
  5. ONGC is the dominant end-customer — Directly or indirectly, ONGC's capex drives SEAMEC's revenue.
  6. The Tonnage Tax Scheme — SEAMEC pays tax based on net tonnage, not profits. Structurally low effective tax rate (6.89%).
  7. Fleet age and condition matter — Older vessels face restrictions and higher maintenance. SEAMEC has been rejuvenating its fleet.
  8. Drydocking is a major cost — Vessels must periodically drydock. This is a cash outflow and a period of zero revenue.
  9. Revenue is USD-denominated — Most contracts are in USD, providing a natural hedge against rupee depreciation.
  10. Working capital can be volatile — Receivables from ONGC and other clients can be slow. Swiber receivables (₹113.47 Cr fully provided) are a reminder of credit risk.
  11. Capital allocation is a key question — SEAMEC holds ₹367 Cr of non-current financial investments while raising debt for vessel acquisitions.
